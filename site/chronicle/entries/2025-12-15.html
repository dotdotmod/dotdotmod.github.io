This year, I've seen a huge influx of people sharing a common vibe about Minecraft and a common phenomenon dubbed "The 2 week phase". Basically, you come back to Minecraft after a long time away, enjoy playing the game a bunch for 2 weeks, slowly run out of steam, then stop playing for a few months before repeating the cycle. It's something that many people have come to hate a bunch, seemingly because they want to have the same kind of infinite joy and comitment that they had when they were younger of constantly playing in the same world for months and months, never getting bored or losing excitment. There have been many ways in which people have tried to combat this, one of which is a concept called "forever worlds", where... you guessed it, they play on this world forever. Never starting a fresh, constantly building on, and on, until they literally can't anymore. It's very simple in concept, but I wonder how many people will actually be willing to do this. I most modern Minecraft updates, the game is structured in a way where the more progress you make, the less challenge there is. In a typical gameplay loop, the player gets stronger and stronger by interacting with the games mechanics and in response, the game also gets harder and more challenging to keep things fun, until the game ends. Minecraft is basically the same. You start with nothing, get wood, go underground, get stone, get iron, get diamonds, go to the nether, find a fortress, get blaze rods, kill endermen, make eyes of ender, go to the stronghold, go to the end, and kill the ender dragon. The End..... or is it? Of course, Minecraft is a game that prides itself on infinite worlds and it's practically endless nature, so what's next? Well, I'd like to discuss my theory on how Minecraft is structured and how it creates a disconnect for many people.

Minecraft is split into two halves that create one full. The Creative side and the Adventure side. On one side, the game is treated like an empty canvas for the game to be painted on, and on the other side is a well built Adventure game, with a steady and challenging progression system that also offers a bit of replayability. Together, these two halves create the brilliant masterpiece that is Minecraft. Neither side can be done on it's own, otherwise an incomplete experience will be had. Therefore, to play Minecraft to it's fullest is to express yourself and your ideas in your world, while advancing through the game's mechanics and becoming a stronger, more powerful player. THIS is the full essence of Minecraft. Keep in mind, I didn't explicitly say "Creative Mode" or "Survival Mode", because I feel like this theory can apply to both actually (Though that's a whole other can of worms I haven't though about enough to really express in written for just yet.. ).

In many videos that talk about Minecraft becoming boring, they reminise on their childhood days of when they could play Minecraft all day without getting bored for a second, always wanting to play, always learning new ways to enjoy the game. They then go on to talk about how something has changed in the game itself that no longer makes them feel this way. This sentiment has become extremely common everywhere around the Minecraft community (though it has been dying out slowly). I've also noticed that those same people tend to play Minecraft in a very specific way, that is playing it from "start" to "finish", starting from gathering wood and making tools all the way to defeating the ender dragon. After this, they usually explore and accomplish all other tangible and objective tasks that Minecraft offers, e.g. getting all advancements, exploring every type of dungeon, defeating the wither and any other bossses, obtaining all the best gear and equipment in the game, collecting every kind of item available, e.t.c. There is absolutely nothing wrong with playing the game like this, infact, like I said earlier, this aspect of the game is extremely important and make Minecraft way more fun than if it was just creative mode only. However, playing the game this way, over, and over, and over again can get EXTREMELY boring. After all, it's just the same gameplay loop over and over again. This is perfectly fine too, a game doesn't NEED to be infinitely replayable. Just take any RPG game ever, and you'll see the same thing. A mostly linear progression from point A to point B. But I think the problem is, that people are disillusioned with the idea that the sole purpose of Minecraft is to be played forever without getting boring, and although it CAN be, the way these people play, simply can't sustain that idea. You'll just get bored eventually. These same people also blame the game itself and the developers for failing to feed into their desire for infinite content, which I think is unfair. Besides that, I think Minecraft constantly expanding and becoming a bigger and bigger game actually doesn't really make it more fun.. atleast on a linear scale.

So what solution do I propose to these people? Well.. obviously just be more creative and whimsical, and stop treating the game as a content machine, and try to interact with the second main half of Minecraft, but honestly I don't think this is a real solution. These people most likely fell in love with Minecraft for different reasons than people like me, so I don't think it's fair to just tell them to just "play more like I do!!!". Different people have different tastes and preferences. That's that. So what's the real solution?? I dunno. Play something else? Many people have gone back to older versions of the game and have found solice there, but I think that stems mostly from a disliking of the direction modern Minecraft as a game is taking, rather than a dislike for what it is and it no longer satisfying their wants. But that's a whole other talk too...

But DotdotMod, why are you arguing with invisible ghosts on the internet who aren't even reading or care about what you have to say? I dunno. I just wanted to get this out. I know all too well that you can never make everyone happy (especially online..) and that some problems can't be solved. So yeah, that's that.
